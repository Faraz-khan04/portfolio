Privacy Policy

Footprint for Android, published on Google Play by Hanito (Mohd Faraz Khan) · Last updated: 21 October 2026

Footprint does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data. The app is not granted the Android internet permission, so it is technically unable to send anything off your device.

What the app records on your device

  • Which apps you use and for how long, and when you unlock your phone.
  • When your screen turns on and off, and which apps send you notifications.
  • Your step count, read from your phone's step-counter sensor and, where available, from the hourly step totals that Google Play services records on your phone (its on-device step recording). Footprint only reads these totals locally; it does not use a Google account and does not send them anywhere.
  • The name, category and icon of the apps you use, so the dashboard can show them.
  • Your own answers to the "was that worth it?" question, and the nudge times worked out from them.
  • If you set app rules: the rules themselves, your answers when an app asks why you're opening it, and whether you closed the app or took extra time on each rule screen.
  • If you use the study timer: each session's subject, start and end time.
  • If you walk for extra time on a rule screen: that you did, using the step sensor while that screen is open.
  • If you use Future You: your goal (its name, how many parts are left, an optional date and your time estimate) and the short notes you write to yourself. They stay on the phone and are deleted with "Delete everything".
  • If you turn on Reels and Shorts blocking: a count of how often a short-video feed was closed, and which app it was in.

The app does not read the contents of notifications, messages, or anything on your screen. It does not use your camera, microphone, location, contacts, or accounts.

Where it is stored

In the app's private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read. Events older than 90 days are deleted automatically. Backups and device-to-device transfer are disabled for this data.

What is shared

Nothing, unless you choose it. The "weekly receipt" is an image you create and share yourself, through Android's normal share sheet, to an app you pick. "Export" writes a file to a location you choose.

Permissions and why

  • Usage access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS): app usage, unlocks, screen and notification events. Without it there is nothing to show.
  • Physical activity (ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION): reading the step-counter sensor and Google Play services' on-device step totals. Optional; the rest works without it.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS): to send you a nudge before a moment you told the app you regret, a reminder to review a few sessions, and a note when a study session ends. Optional.
  • Display over other apps (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW): to show the full-screen page over an app when a rule you set is reached, or while a study session you started is running. Optional; only needed for app rules.
  • Foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE) and start at boot (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED): keep your app rules and study timer working, shown as an ongoing notification. It runs only while you have rules set or a session running.
  • View network connections (ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) and keep awake (WAKE_LOCK): added by Android's own background-task library (WorkManager), which the app uses to run its 15-minute update. It can only tell whether a connection exists; without the internet permission the app still cannot send or receive anything.

Reels and Shorts blocking (Accessibility service)

Some versions of Footprint can close YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Snapchat Spotlight for you. This uses Android's Accessibility service, which you turn on yourself after reading an explanation in the app and agreeing to it. It receives information only from those three apps, only while they are on screen. It looks at the names of the screen's parts (such as "reel player") and which tab is selected, and presses Back when a feed you blocked opens. It does not read messages, captions, comments or anything you type. Nothing it sees is stored, apart from a count of how often it closed a feed, and nothing is sent anywhere. You can turn it off at any time in Android's Accessibility settings.

Your control

In Settings you can hide any app, which stops it being recorded and deletes its history immediately; turn off step or notification collection; and choose how long history is kept, from 30 days to a year.

"Export JSON" and "Export CSV" give you a copy of everything. "Delete everything" removes all stored events, app information and settings immediately. Uninstalling the app also removes all of it.

Children

Footprint is not directed at children under 13 and collects no data from anyone.

Changes

If a future version ever adds anything that leaves your device, this policy will be updated first, and that feature will stay off until you turn it on.
